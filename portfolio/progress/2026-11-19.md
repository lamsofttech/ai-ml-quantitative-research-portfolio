## Conditioning result

The estimated Lebesgue constant on the 401-point grid grows as follows:

| Degree | Equidistant | Chebyshev-Lobatto |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 2.208 | 1.799 |
| 8 | 10.941 | 2.275 |
| 12 | 89.253 | 2.539 |
| 16 | 933.090 | 2.724 |

At degree 16, the equidistant interpolation design has a roughly 343-times
larger measured worst-case amplification bound. In plain language, some pattern
of small errors in supplied node values can be magnified far more strongly by
the equidistant polynomial. The Lebesgue constant is a bound, not a claim that
every perturbation receives that amplification.

The prior Runge result remains unchanged: Chebyshev-Lobatto nodes reduced the
degree-16 measured maximum approximation error by about 391 times. Together,
the results distinguish what happened for one known function from the more
general sensitivity caused by node placement.
